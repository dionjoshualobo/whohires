## What open source<br /><span class="accent">actually is.</span>

<div class="split">
  <div>
    <div class="definition">
      Open source is software whose source code is public and licensed so anyone can <strong>use, study, change and share</strong> it.
    </div>
    <div class="quote">A license grants freedom. It does not pay the maintainers.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="pillar-list">
    <div class="pillar">
      <div class="pillar-num">01</div>
      <div>
        <h4>License</h4>
        <p>The legal permission to use, modify and redistribute the code.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="pillar">
      <div class="pillar-num">02</div>
      <div>
        <h4>Community</h4>
        <p>Users, contributors and maintainers who shape the project over time.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="pillar">
      <div class="pillar-num">03</div>
      <div>
        <h4>Maintenance</h4>
        <p>Releases, security, support, documentation and the work nobody sees.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

  <div>

## If the code is free,<br /><span class="accent">where does the money come from?</span>

<p class="subhead">Open source does not mean the business has to be free. Companies charge for <strong>expertise, reliability, convenience and accountability</strong> — not for the source code itself.</p>

<div class="model-list">
  <div class="model-row">
    <div class="model-index">01</div>
    <div class="model-title">Enterprise support</div>
    <div class="model-desc">Paid support, consulting, maintenance and enterprise-grade services around the software.</div>
    <div class="model-tag">Red Hat · RHEL</div>
  </div>
  <div class="model-row">
    <div class="model-index">02</div>
    <div class="model-title">Hosted services</div>
    <div class="model-desc">The software is open, but users pay someone to host, manage and maintain it.</div>
    <div class="model-tag">Frappe Cloud</div>
  </div>
  <div class="model-row">
    <div class="model-index">03</div>
    <div class="model-title">Open core</div>
    <div class="model-desc">The core product is open; advanced enterprise features and controls are paid.</div>
    <div class="model-tag">GitLab</div>
  </div>
  <div class="model-row">
    <div class="model-index">04</div>
    <div class="model-title">Dual licensing</div>
    <div class="model-desc">A free community license plus a paid commercial license for different terms.</div>
    <div class="model-tag">MySQL · Qt</div>
  </div>
  <div class="model-row">
    <div class="model-index">05</div>
    <div class="model-title">Sponsorship &amp; grants</div>
    <div class="model-desc">Companies and foundations fund developers, events and maintenance directly.</div>
    <div class="model-tag">GitHub Sponsors</div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="callout"><strong>Transition:</strong> if companies make money around open source, they need people to build, document, support, design and grow those projects.</div>

## Four myths that<br /><span class="accent">keep beginners out.</span>

<div class="myth-list">
  <div class="myth-row">
    <div class="myth-num">01</div>
    <div class="myth-claim">“I need to be a senior developer first.”</div>
    <div class="myth-reality"><strong>No.</strong> Projects need docs, triage, design, translation, testing and community work just as much as code.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="myth-row">
    <div class="myth-num">02</div>
    <div class="myth-claim">“I need permission to start.”</div>
    <div class="myth-reality"><strong>No.</strong> Read the contribution guide, start with a small useful action, and ask questions in the open.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="myth-row">
    <div class="myth-num">03</div>
    <div class="myth-claim">“A contribution means a merged PR.”</div>
    <div class="myth-reality"><strong>No.</strong> A reproducible bug report, a docs fix or a helpful answer is real, visible work.</div>
  </div>
  <div class="myth-row">
    <div class="myth-num">04</div>
    <div class="myth-claim">“AI can do all of this now.”</div>
    <div class="myth-reality"><strong>No.</strong> AI raises the volume of output, not the level of trust. Judgment, verification and accountability still matter.</div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="quote">The bar is not “know everything.” The bar is “be useful, be specific, be easy to work with.”</div>

## Six ways in.<br /><span class="accent">Pick one lane.</span>

<p class="subhead">You do not need to try all six. Start with the one closest to what you already do well.</p>

<div class="pathway-grid">
  <div class="pathway-row">
    <div class="pathway-num">01</div>
    <div>
      <h4>Documentation &amp; technical writing</h4>
      <p>Fix setup steps, write tutorials, explain a confusing feature.</p>
      <div class="pathway-first">Rewrite one failing install step.</div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="pathway-row">
    <div class="pathway-num">02</div>
    <div>
      <h4>Translation &amp; localization</h4>
      <p>Make interfaces and guides useful to more languages and communities.</p>
      <div class="pathway-first">Fix one misleading translated string.</div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="pathway-row">
    <div class="pathway-num">03</div>
    <div>
      <h4>UX &amp; accessibility</h4>
      <p>Audit user journeys, create mockups and remove a11y barriers.</p>
      <div class="pathway-first">File one issue with a screenshot.</div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="pathway-row">
    <div class="pathway-num">04</div>
    <div>
      <h4>Testing &amp; triage</h4>
      <p>Reproduce bugs, request missing logs and verify fixes.</p>
      <div class="pathway-first">Reproduce one needs-reproduction issue.</div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="pathway-row">
    <div class="pathway-num">05</div>
    <div>
      <h4>Community &amp; DevRel</h4>
      <p>Answer questions, welcome newcomers and write release notes.</p>
      <div class="pathway-first">Answer one unanswered forum post.</div>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="pathway-row">
    <div class="pathway-num">06</div>
    <div>
      <h4>Research &amp; product</h4>
      <p>Collect feedback, propose features and prioritize real needs.</p>
      <div class="pathway-first">Turn five complaints into one issue.</div>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

## Make your first<br /><span class="accent">useful contribution.</span>

<div class="steps">
  <div class="steps-list">
    <div class="step">
      <div class="step-num">01</div>
      <div>
        <h4>Pick a tool you actually use</h4>
        <p>Familiarity gives you context. Context makes you useful.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="step">
      <div class="step-num">02</div>
      <div>
        <h4>Read CONTRIBUTING.md and the code of conduct</h4>
        <p>Every project has rules. They change. Read the current ones.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="step">
      <div class="step-num">03</div>
      <div>
        <h4>Find the right surface</h4>
        <p>Docs, issues, discussions, translations, design or community channels.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="step">
      <div class="step-num">04</div>
      <div>
        <h4>Start small, specific and verifiable</h4>
        <p>One broken link fixed beats a vague offer to “help with docs.”</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="step">
      <div class="step-num">05</div>
      <div>
        <h4>Write for the next beginner</h4>
        <p>Explain what you wish someone had told you on day one.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="step">
      <div class="step-num">06</div>
      <div>
        <h4>Follow up and close the loop</h4>
        <p>Respond, revise and thank reviewers. That is the actual skill.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="bezel starter">
    <div class="bezel-inner">
      <div class="tag">starter kit</div>
      <h4>Your first 30 minutes</h4>
      <ul>
        <li>Open the repo</li>
        <li>Read CONTRIBUTING.md</li>
        <li>Search <code>good first issue</code></li>
        <li>Pick one docs or triage task</li>
        <li>Leave one clear comment</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="callout"><strong>Your first contribution does not have to be a pull request.</strong> It could be a bug report that saves a developer three hours.</div>

## Where the work<br /><span class="accent">is hiding.</span>

<p class="subhead">Issue labels are a map. Learn what they mean and you can find useful work in almost any active project.</p>

<div class="label-grid">
  <div class="label-row">
    <code>good first issue</code>
    <p>Scoped, newcomer-friendly tasks with a clear exit.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="label-row">
    <code>documentation</code>
    <p>Setup, tutorials, reference fixes and missing examples.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="label-row">
    <code>needs-reproduction</code>
    <p>Bugs waiting for someone to confirm and document steps.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="label-row">
    <code>help wanted</code>
    <p>Maintainers have explicitly asked for outside help.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="label-row">
    <code>accessibility</code>
    <p>Keyboard, contrast, screen-reader and semantic fixes.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="label-row">
    <code>translations</code>
    <p>Localization work that makes software useful to more people.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="label-row">
    <code>beginner-friendly</code>
    <p>Explicit signals that questions and slow progress are welcome.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="label-row">
    <code>design</code>
    <p>UX reviews, mockups, flow audits and interface improvements.</p>
  </div>

## What Frappe<br /><span class="accent">actually rewards.</span>

<div class="split-wide">
  <div class="action-list">
    <div class="action-row">
      <div class="action-num">01</div>
      <div>
        <h4>Improve the documentation</h4>
        <p>Frappe Wiki is a real documentation surface. Fix setup steps and explain workflows clearly.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="action-row">
      <div class="action-num">02</div>
      <div>
        <h4>Report reproducible bugs</h4>
        <p>What happened, what you expected, exact steps, versions and relevant logs.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="action-row">
      <div class="action-num">03</div>
      <div>
        <h4>Help in the forum</h4>
        <p>Answer one question with a link and a clear next step. Make the next person’s day easier.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
    <div class="action-row">
      <div class="action-num">04</div>
      <div>
        <h4>Translate and test</h4>
        <p>Localize interfaces and verify fixes on real deployments.</p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="bezel rules">
    <div class="bezel-inner">
      <div class="tag">check before you start</div>
      <h4>PRs are gated.</h4>
      <p>Frappe suspended community pull requests in March 2026 after a surge of AI-generated, resume-driven submissions.</p>
      <ul>
        <li>Docs, bug reports and forum help remain open</li>
        <li>PRs need a vouch or maintainer coordination</li>
        <li>Always read the current CONTRIBUTING.md</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="quote">Check the current rules before you invest hours. The rules changed for a reason.</div>

## Start small.<br /><span class="accent">Stay visible.</span>

<div class="roadmap">
  <div class="roadmap-step">
    <div class="roadmap-dot"></div>
    <h4>Discover</h4>
    <p>Find a project you actually use or care about.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="roadmap-step">
    <div class="roadmap-dot"></div>
    <h4>Contribute</h4>
    <p>Make one small, useful, verifiable contribution.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="roadmap-step">
    <div class="roadmap-dot"></div>
    <h4>Repeat</h4>
    <p>Become a familiar, reliable presence.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="roadmap-step">
    <div class="roadmap-dot"></div>
    <h4>Connect</h4>
    <p>Build trust with maintainers and peers.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="roadmap-step">
    <div class="roadmap-dot"></div>
    <h4>Grow</h4>
    <p>Turn public proof into opportunities.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="tonight">
  <div>
    <h3>Do this tonight</h3>
    <p>Thirty minutes is enough to start.</p>
  </div>
  <ol>
    <li>Pick one tool you already use daily.</li>
    <li>Open its repo and read <code>CONTRIBUTING.md</code>.</li>
    <li>Find one docs, triage or accessibility task and leave one clear comment.</li>
  </ol>
</div>
